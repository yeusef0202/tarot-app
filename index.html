How tarot works

The deck

A tarot deck has 78 cards split into two groups:

  • Major Arcana (22 cards), numbered 0 (The Fool) to XXI (The World). They represent big life lessons, turning points and spiritual themes, often read as "The Fool's Journey" through life.
  • Minor Arcana (56 cards), four suits of 14 cards (Ace to Ten, plus Page, Knight, Queen and King). They describe everyday events, feelings and people.

The four suits

Wands · Fire: passion, creativity, ambition, action.
Cups · Water: emotions, love, relationships, intuition.
Swords · Air: thoughts, communication, truth, conflict.
Pentacles · Earth: money, work, health, the material world.

Yes / No questions

If your question can be answered with yes or no ("Will I pass my test tomorrow?"), the app gives an answer at the end of the reading. Each card leans yes, no or maybe: bright cards like The Sun, The Star or the Nine of Cups (the "wish card") are a strong yes, while The Tower, the Three of Swords or the Ten of Swords are a strong no. A reversed card weakens and flips its lean. The cards are weighted by position (the Future and Outcome cards count most) to reach one of five answers: Yes, Likely yes, Unclear, Likely no or No.

Tip: keep reversed cards switched on for yes/no questions. Most upright cards lean yes, so without reversals the answers skew positive.

Numbers carry meaning too: Aces are beginnings, Fives are challenges, Tens are completion. Court cards often represent people or personality traits (Pages are students and messengers, Knights are action, Queens are inner mastery, Kings are outer mastery).

Reversed cards

When a card appears upside-down it is reversed. A reversal usually means the card's energy is blocked, delayed, internalized or excessive, and it is not simply "the opposite". Many readers ignore reversals; you can switch them off in the Reading menu.

Doing a reading

  1. Choose a spread. Each position in a spread has its own meaning (for example Past, Present and Future).
  2. Focus on a question. Open questions ("What should I know about…?") work better than yes/no ones.
  3. Shuffle the deck while thinking about your question, and optionally cut it.
  4. Pick cards from the fanned-out deck, one for each position, in order.
  5. Read each card in the light of its position, then look at the reading as a whole: many Major Arcana, a dominant suit or lots of reversals all tell a story.

Tarot is a tool for reflection and self-exploration. It is not a substitute for medical, legal, financial or psychological advice.
